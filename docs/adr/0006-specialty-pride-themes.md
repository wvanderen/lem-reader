# Specialty pride themes ride the chrome ribbon hooks

The reader asked for two specialty themes — Trans pride (light) and Bi pride (dark) — that carry their flag's colors into UI elements, with the reading progress bar named as the natural host. The Wayfinder system (ADR 0005) bans gradients anywhere: enamel signage is flat painted metal. Rather than weaken that rule or refuse the rooms, the flag rides a narrow, explicitly-tokened exception: the "wash hooks" (`--progress-fill`, `--lit-underline`; `--band-edge` stays a semantic slot) default to the solid Wayfinder materials in `:root`, and only the two specialty `[data-theme]` blocks override them. ONE gradient material paints both moments: `--flag-wash`, a real soft blend of the flag's hues, rides the dynamic progress hairline (background) and the lit current-location underline (border-image). Two earlier shapes failed in the real browser and are recorded as refusals: a hard-stop flag ribbon fragmented into aliasing when the fill's `transform: scaleX` compressed it; kept on the band's bottom edge it stacked block color 1px above the wash — a clashing double-flag (worst in bi-dark); and kept as the lit underline's mark it read as chunky confetti chips at board scale (three on bi, five near-invisible stripes on trans). The band edge stays the basic lit metal, the article measure stays untouched — the Brightest Panel rule holds.

Both rooms are hand-authored presets, not custom-slot derivations: the theme enum widens additively (`trans-light`, `bi-dark` — the same no-schemaVersion-bump discipline as #86/#120), the pre-paint mirror map learns both keys, and every policed pair is pinned by tests (`prideThemes.test.ts` audits the full text/rule contract; the highlight AA extraction map extends to both blocks). The band edge and lit underline keep their solid color painted beneath the `border-image`, and forced-colors strips the image — the gradient never carries meaning, only identity.
