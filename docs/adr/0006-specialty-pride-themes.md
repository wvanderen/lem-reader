# Specialty pride themes ride the chrome ribbon hooks

The reader asked for two specialty themes — Trans pride (light) and Bi pride (dark) — that carry their flag's colors into UI elements, with the reading progress bar named as the natural host. The Wayfinder system (ADR 0005) bans gradients anywhere: enamel signage is flat painted metal. Rather than weaken that rule or refuse the rooms, the two flags ride a narrow, explicitly-tokened exception: three chrome "ribbon hooks" (`--progress-fill`, `--band-edge`, `--lit-underline`) default to the solid Wayfinder materials in `:root`, and only the two specialty `[data-theme]` blocks override them. Each theme ships TWO gradient materials with separate jobs: `--flag-ribbon`, the crisp hard-stop flag in true geometry (trans: equal fifths blue/pink/white/pink/blue; bi: 40/20/40 pink/purple/blue), flies on the static frame — the band's edge and the current-location underline; `--flag-wash`, a real soft blend of the flag's hues, rides the dynamic indicator — the progress hairline. The split fixes two real failures of a single ribbon: the fill animates via `transform: scaleX`, so its paint compresses with progress and hard stripes fragment into aliasing at low fill; and the hairline sits 1px under the band edge, where two identical crisp ribbons stacked into one confused double-flag. The article measure stays untouched — the Brightest Panel rule holds.

Both rooms are hand-authored presets, not custom-slot derivations: the theme enum widens additively (`trans-light`, `bi-dark` — the same no-schemaVersion-bump discipline as #86/#120), the pre-paint mirror map learns both keys, and every policed pair is pinned by tests (`prideThemes.test.ts` audits the full text/rule contract; the highlight AA extraction map extends to both blocks). The band edge and lit underline keep their solid color painted beneath the `border-image`, and forced-colors strips the image — the gradient never carries meaning, only identity.
